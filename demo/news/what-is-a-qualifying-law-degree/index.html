What does “Qualifying Law Degree” actually mean?

The term that opens the door to the Bar and the CVLE — and how UoME’s two LLBs and the GDL fit.

Key takeaways
  • A Qualifying Law Degree meets the academic stage of legal training in a jurisdiction.
  • UoME’s two LLBs are Qualifying Law Degrees for England & Wales and Mauritius.
  • No law degree yet? The one- or two-year GDL is the conversion route.

What the term means

A Qualifying Law Degree is a law degree that meets the academic stage of legal training in a jurisdiction. UoME’s LLB (Hons) with English and Mauritian Law and LLB (Hons) with English Law are both Qualifying Law Degrees for the purpose of practice in England & Wales and Mauritius.

The next step in England & Wales

The next stage is professional: the Bar Professional Course (BPC) if you want to become a barrister, or the Legal Practice Course (LPC) if you want to become a solicitor. Both lead to practice in England & Wales only.

The route in Mauritius

In Mauritius the degree is recognised by the Council for Vocational Legal Education (CVLE) for admission to the vocational examination conducted under its supervision.

If your first degree is not in law

If you already hold a degree in another subject, the Graduate Diploma in Law (GDL) is the conversion route. It is one year full-time or two years part-time, and also carries recognition for the BPC, LPC and CVLE route. Applicants who want to train as a barrister in England & Wales should apply for a Certificate of Academic Standing (COAS) from the Bar Standards Board, ideally before applying.
